# Timebox as a session-scoped Plan property

Dragging an Outcome onto the day timeline (#40) needs a domain representation. The candidates were a first-class Timebox entity (multiple slots per Outcome, scheduling beyond the current session), a scheduled date-time on the Outcome row itself (the old `state: scheduled` framing), or a property of the FocusSession Plan entry.

We chose the **Plan-entry property**: a Timebox is a scheduled start + duration on the (FocusSession, Outcome) Plan membership — at most one per entry, created or edited during Planning, adjustable during Execution, and always dying with its session. Review's `rollover` Disposition carries the commitment into the next Planning, never the Timebox; scheduling is redone fresh against each day's calendar. Automated chunking (a 60-minute Timebox rendered as sprint blocks) is purely presentational, consistent with Sprints/Breaks never persisting.

A Timebox is an *intention about when within a session*, which is exactly the Plan's scope — so it inherits the Plan's lifecycle for free instead of needing its own (what would an orphaned Timebox mean after Review?). It adds no new state axis: an Outcome with a Timebox keeps its Intent and List memberships, which kept the implicit-List model intact and dissolved the `state: scheduled` conceptual error. Auto-carrying a slot into the next session would schedule against a day the user hasn't reviewed — planning is a deliberate act (same principle as ADR-0002). The costs accepted: one slot per Outcome per session, and no scheduling for future days (future-day commitments are the hard landscape's job — see ADR-0014).
